From the tower to the river

Five places, from the Clérigos tower down to the Douro and the Dom Luís I bridge.

Open this route on the 3D map

  1. Clérigos Church and Tower

    Stop 1 of 5 · 1763

    In 1731, under dean Jerónimo de Távora e Noronha, the design of a new church was commissioned from Niccolò Nasoni, a Tuscan who had reached Portugal by way of Malta and was then working on Porto Cathedral.

    The church was built in 1732–1749 and the tower in 1754–1763.

    It was the tower, more than the church, that became Porto's landmark.

    In the real city

    The stair is narrow and spiral, all 225 steps, so wear comfortable shoes. The view is best at sunset, when Porto and the Douro are lit up.

  2. São Bento Railway Station

    Stop 2 of 5 · 1916

    São Bento station was built on the site of the old Benedictine monastery of São Bento de Avé Maria, demolished in the late 19th century.

    The vestibule holds around 20,000 azulejos covering some 551 square metres.

    Because it is a terminus, trains enter and leave through tunnels.

    In the real city

    The panels are easiest to study from the upper gallery level. The Clérigos tower and the cathedral are a five-minute walk away.

  3. Porto Cathedral (Sé)

    Stop 3 of 5 · 12th century

    Construction began in the first half of the 12th century and continued into the early 13th.

    Niccolò Nasoni added the Baroque lateral gallery around 1736; a new portal followed around 1772.

    John I and Philippa of Lancaster married here in 1387.

    It has been a national monument since 1910.

    In the real city

    The cathedral sits on a hill, so walk up from São Bento station or take the Guindais funicular from Ribeira.

  4. Cais da Ribeira

    Stop 4 of 5 · 18th century

    The present character of the quarter took shape in the 17th and 18th centuries: tall, narrow houses with arcades on the ground floor, tiled roofs and façades covered in azulejo tiles.

    By the 20th century the quarter had grown poor and dilapidated, but restoration began in the second half of the century.

    In 1996 the historic centre of Porto, including Ribeira and the Dom Luís I Bridge, was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage list.

    In the real city

    Come early in the morning before the crowds: the arcades and coloured façades are best appreciated then. Pleasure boats leave from Praça da Ribeira, and the Gaia cable car runs nearby.

  5. Dom Luís I Bridge

    Stop 5 of 5 · 1886

    The idea of a new crossing over the Douro emerged in the 1870s, when the old suspension bridge, the Ponte Pênsil, could no longer cope with the growing city.

    The design was by Théophile Seyrig, a former partner of Gustave Eiffel.

    Its 172 m central arch was a record among metal bridges of its time.

    In the real city

    Climb to the upper deck at sunset: the view over Ribeira, the Serra do Pilar monastery and the Douro valley is at its best.
