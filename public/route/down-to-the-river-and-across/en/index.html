Down to the river and across

Five places by the Douro, from the Church of São Francisco to the Serra do Pilar monastery on the south bank.

Open this route on the 3D map

  1. Church of São Francisco

    Stop 1 of 5 · 1383

    In 1383, by decree of King Ferdinand, a special protector of the Franciscans, work began on the present church, completed in 1410.

    It is the finest example of Gothic architecture in Porto; under King John I a rare mural, the Madonna of the Rose, attributed to António Florentim, was painted here.

    Beneath it lie catacombs holding the remains of friars, now open as part of the museum.

    In the real city

    The church is steps from the Stock Exchange Palace, so visit the two together. Inside it is dark and very gold — avoid flash and let your eyes adjust.

  2. Palácio da Bolsa (Stock Exchange Palace)

    Stop 2 of 5 · 1842

    The Nations' Courtyard (Pátio das Nações) is roofed in glass and decorated with the coats of arms of the countries Porto traded with; around it lie the Golden Room, the Assembly Rooms and the Portrait Room.

    Six architects worked on the project, including José Marques da Silva.

    It is the headquarters of the Porto Commercial Association.

    In the real city

    It sits right beside the Church of São Francisco in Ribeira, so the two are easy to visit back to back.

  3. Cais da Ribeira

    Stop 3 of 5 · 18th century

    Trade between Porto and Gaia thrived here as early as the Middle Ages, and the Douro was the main route for wine, salt and goods.

    Praça da Ribeira is the historic heart of the quarter, with its market and fountain.

    17th–18th-century arcaded houses and azulejo façades define the quay.

    In the real city

    Pleasure boats leave from Praça da Ribeira, and the Gaia cable car runs nearby.

  4. Dom Luís I Bridge

    Stop 4 of 5 · 1886

    The design was by Théophile Seyrig, a former partner of Gustave Eiffel.

    Inaugurated on 31 October 1886; the first stone was laid on 21 November 1881.

    In 1996 the bridge was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage list.

    In the real city

    Climb to the upper deck at sunset: the view over Ribeira, the Serra do Pilar monastery and the Douro valley is at its best.

  5. Monastery of Serra do Pilar

    Stop 5 of 5 · 1672

    Construction began in 1538 (some sources say 1537); the new church started in 1597 and was re-inaugurated on 17 July 1672.

    The church and cloister are both circular and of the same diameter — the only such case in Portugal.

    A UNESCO World Heritage site since 1996, with Porto's historic centre and the Luís I bridge.

    In the real city

    The best time to climb to the monastery is early morning or sunset, when the terrace gives a panorama of Porto, the Ribeira and the Luís I bridge.
